# The Last Lighthouse Keeper

The fog had been rolling off the Atlantic for three days now, thick enough to swallow sound whole. Elias stood at the edge of the cliffside cottage, his knuckles white around the railing, watching the beam sweep through nothingness.

At seventy-two, he'd kept the lighthouse running longer than anyone expected him to. Longer than the town council expected him to. Long enough that most people forgot he still lived here.

A knock came at the door—three sharp raps, followed by silence.

Elias frowned. No one visited out here anymore. Not since the highway rerouted past the coast, not since the automated buoys took over the shipping lanes. He walked inside, the floorboards groaning familiar complaints under his weight.

The woman standing in the doorway couldn't have been more than twenty. Rainwater dripped from her coat onto the welcome mat. Her eyes were wide, scanning the room filled with ticking clocks and faded photographs.

"I'm looking for the keeper," she said.

Elias smiled faintly. "That's me."

She blinked. "You're the last one?"

"I am."

"Why did you stay?"

He looked toward the window, where the light swept through the fog again, painting the walls in pale gold. "Because someone has to watch the ships. Even if there aren't many left."

She stepped closer, pulling a crumpled map from her pocket. It showed a route marked in red ink, ending right at the headland. "My grandfather told me stories about this place. Said the light saved his ship during a storm forty years ago. He wanted me to come say thank you."

Elias felt something crack open behind his ribs. He hadn't thought of storms in decades. He hadn't thought of ships at all.

"You should go home soon," he said quietly. "The weather will turn tonight."

As she turned to leave, she paused. "Will you ever stop keeping the light?"

Elias watched her disappear down the stone steps, then returned to the lantern room. His hands moved automatically, checking the oil level, polishing the glass, winding the clockwork mechanism that made the great lens rotate.

Outside, the fog began to thin. And somewhere far below, a horn sounded—a deep, resonant note that echoed against the cliffs. A ship, passing safely through the channel.

Elias smiled. Someone was still watching.
